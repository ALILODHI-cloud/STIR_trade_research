## Does “flatten” mean “invert more”?

Under **back − front**:

| State of gap | Flatten means | Invert more? |
|---|---|---|
| Steep (+ve) | Spread falls toward 0 | Not yet — just less steep |
| Flat (~0) | Spread goes negative | Yes — start inverting |
| Already inverted (−ve) | Spread more negative | **Yes** |

So: flatten always = back−front *down*. Invert more is the special case when
you are at or through zero. **M7M8 is already inverted**, so GS’s “flatten
M7M8” *is* “invert more” on this gap.

Trade expression (rate space): short M27 / long M28 (pay front, receive back)
profits if M28−M27 falls.

## Rigorous read of the Nov-hike → M7M8-flatten claim

### What has to be true for the trade to work

A **parallel** +25 bp lift of the whole policy path leaves M28−M27 unchanged.
Flattening needs the hike to be priced as **raising the path through mid-27
more than mid-28** — i.e. bring forward / raise the peak without a matching
lift in 2028 (classic “one more hike, terminal roughly capped”).

That *can* work when:

- An extra near hike is *not* yet in the strip, and
- The post-peak flank is willing to stay anchored.

### What the strip already says

1. **Hikes are largely in the front.** Z26−spot = +52 bp. Even allowing for
   RFR/term premium, that is order **~2 × 25 bp** of cumulative tightening by
   the Dec-26 quarter. Peak at U27 is +115 bp ≈ **4–5 steps** from spot. A
   single November 25 bp move is not a fresh shock to M27/M28 — it is mostly
   *which meeting* gets the step, not *whether* the path gets there.

2. **M7M8 sits *behind* the peak.** Peak is U27 (4.88%). M27 is already on
   the shoulder; M28 is further down the flank. The aggressive steepening is
   *in front of* M27 (Z26→M27 still +57 bp). GS picked a gap that has **already
   done** the easy invert; the residual is −5 bp with −31.5 bp as the YTD floor.

3. **Their own MPC read fights the trade.** “Limited urgency,” restrictive
   policy, muted passthrough — that is the scenario where *November drops out*
   and front-end hike premium bleeds. Bleed typically **steepens** gaps past
   the peak (M28−M27 rises toward zero / positive) — opposite of the flattener.
   They name energy downside as the risk; the MPC wording is the same risk
   without needing oil.

4. **QT does not help M7M8.** Their Exhibit 9 story is 10y term-premium
   compression. SONIA 3M M27/M28 are policy-path contracts. Gilt bull-flatten
   and STIR front-end flatten are different trades; bundling them is rhetoric,
   not a hedge ratio.

### Verdict

| Piece | Assessment |
|---|---|
| QT → lower long Gilt yields / richer ASW | Separate book; not tested here |
| Nov hike still GS base case | Asserted; MPC text in the *same note* is soft |
| Nov hike → flatten M7M8 | Mechanically needs *differential* path lift; strip already has ~4–5 steps to peak |
| M7M8 as the expression | Already −5 bp inverted, mid-YTD, shallow vs U27/U28 or Z27/Z28 |
| Skew | Wrong-way if Nov is delayed/cancelled (GS’s own soft-MPC paragraph) |

**Net:** as a *SONIA* idea, “flatten M7M8 into a November hike” is weak on the
2026-09-18 strip. The hike path into mid-27 is mostly priced; the gap is
already inverted and not cheap versus its own history; the note’s MPC language
is the wrong-way risk. If you want the *shape* thesis (peak holds, 2028
softens), **U27U28 or Z27Z28** (back−front already −14 / −13.5 bp) are the
gaps that have actually inverted — but they are also further into that move.
A cleaner expression of “one more near hike, terminal capped” would be a
**front steepener into the peak** (e.g. Z26H27 or H27M27 still steep at
+38 / +19 bp) paired with a view on *where* the peak sits — not paying for
more inversion in M7M8 from −5.

## Flatten / invert cheat sheet (this book)

- Spread = **back − front** (bp).
- Flatten = spread ↓.
- On an inverted gap (M7M8 = −5): flatten further = **invert more**. Yes.
