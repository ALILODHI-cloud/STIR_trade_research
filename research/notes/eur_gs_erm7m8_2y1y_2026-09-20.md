## 4. 2y1y longs — only with the *peak* as hedge

| Structure | Aug 1 → now | Aug selloff-day mean |
|---|---:|---:|
| Recv 2y1y / **pay Z26** (front hedge) | **−27.5** | **−1.45** |
| Recv 2y1y / **pay U27** (peak hedge) | **+12.0** | **+1.61** |
| Short ERM7M8 | −0.5 | +0.36 |
| Short Z7Z8 | +10.5 | +1.31 |

Outright 2y1y long (no hedge) is just long U28–U29 rates — those **sold 50–60
bp** since Aug with the strip. GS’s “we don’t expect front-end relief” plus
“long 2y1y” only works if 2y1y **lags** the hike path. Empirically it lags
**U27**, not **Z26**.

## 5. Verdict on their paragraph

| Claim | Data |
|---|---|
| ECB week: higher yields | **True** |
| ECB week: ERM7M8 flatter | **False** (+1.5 steepen) |
| Front-end opening protects 2y1y vs **front** | **False since Aug** (β&gt;1) |
| Peak opening protects 2y1y / M8 | **True** (β≈0.64) |
| ERM7M8 flatten from −4.5 | **OK entry (47th %ile)**, **soft** recent β vs Z7Z8 |
| Long 2y1y vs short U26/Z26 | **Bad** — same failed family |

**Net:** They named the **right calendar family** (M7M8 / post-peak flatten)
and the **wrong hedge** if “2y1y long” is paired with the *very* front-end.
Prefer **pay M27 / recv M28** as a *milder, better-skewed* cousin of short
Z7Z8 — not because last week proved it, and not as a 2y1y vs Z26 pack.
Size for only ~0.4 bp flatten per front-selloff day (Aug sample), vs ~1.3
for Z7Z8; you are buying *room* (13 bp to YTD floor) not *realised beta*.
